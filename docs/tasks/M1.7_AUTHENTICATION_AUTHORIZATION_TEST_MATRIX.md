## 6. Verification Principles

- Treat every grant as conditional, not role-only.
- Deny by default when identity, account status, permission, assignment, scope, resource facts, field classification, workflow state, separation facts, assurance, or policy version is missing/unknown.
- Test both allow and deny paths; an allow-only test does not prove authorization.
- Change one relevant factor at a time for diagnostic matrix rows.
- Test policy logic and representative delivery/database/browser boundaries.
- Do not prove authorization only through hidden UI.
- Assert exact safe outcome, state change, projection, cache/error behavior, and audit effect.
- Use server-owned fixtures and inspect database effects rather than trusting response text alone.
- Use controlled clocks/barriers instead of sleeps.
- Use independent synthetic identities/records/scopes per test worker.
- Tests must be order independent, repeatable, and safe in parallel unless explicitly serialized for a documented database reason.
- A skipped/quarantined/focused critical authentication, authorization, audit, or integrity test blocks the M1 gate.

Do not parse prose Markdown as the runtime authorization source of truth. The accepted machine-readable catalogs are authoritative for execution; traceability proves they still match the approved specifications.

## 9. Authentication Matrix

### 9.1 Account type and state

Test every applicable combination of:

- Principal type: candidate, staff, service.
- Account state: invited/incomplete, active, locked, disabled, closed.
- Email state: unverified/verified.
- Staff MFA state: unenrolled/incomplete/enrolled/reset-required.
- Session state: absent, temporary activation/challenge, active, expired, revoked, stale authorization version.

Prove only eligible combinations resolve an application principal and that candidate/staff/service routes remain isolated.

### 9.2 Candidate authentication

Cover:

- Controlled registration intent type, email binding, expiry, consumption, and concurrency.
- Server-forced candidate account type.
- Duplicate/normalized email behavior without enumeration.
- Verification delivery/resend/supersession/expiry/single use.
- Sign-in before/after verification and across account states.
- Generic recovery request, reset purpose/expiry/single use, and password-change behavior.
- Session rotation/revocation after sign-in, recovery, password change, disable, and sign-out actions.
- Owned-session listing/revocation with masked exact projections.
- Candidate routes rejecting staff/service accounts.

### 9.3 Staff authentication

Cover:

- Invitation-only creation, email binding, issue/resend/revoke/expire/supersede/accept, and concurrency.
- No generic/candidate path creates or converts staff.
- Password plus mandatory TOTP before active principal resolution.
- Temporary first-factor/MFA challenge state is not an application session.
- TOTP valid/invalid/replayed/out-of-window and bounded attempt behavior.
- Trusted-device, email OTP, and SMS OTP cannot bypass the accepted policy.
- Backup-code single use, replay failure, regeneration invalidation, and assurance strength.
- MFA enrollment/reset/recovery, session/code/factor invalidation, and reenrollment.
- Staff recovery cannot use candidate self-service.
- Staff routes reject candidate/service/incomplete/password-only accounts.

### 9.4 Recent authentication

Test exact controlled-clock boundaries for:

- Fresh and expired assurance.
- Required primary/MFA method strength.
- Backup-code restrictions.
- Account, session, purpose, and policy binding.
- Session/role/account/security change invalidation.
- Client-submitted assurance fields ignored.
- Reauthentication followed by a changed role/account/resource state causes complete reauthorization.

## 10. Role and Permission Matrix

At policy level, execute every role against every permission in the accepted catalog.

For each role-permission pair:

- Assert the catalog's explicit maximum grant or denial.
- When granted, add required valid scope/state/separation/assurance facts and prove allow.
- Remove the permission while keeping other facts valid and prove deny.
- Keep the role but request a distinct action such as edit/approve/download/export/admin and prove no implicit inheritance.
- Test retired/unknown role and permission codes deny.
- Test multi-role union only combines explicit grants and cannot bypass separation rules.
- Confirm the chosen allow decision records the least-privileged sufficient effective assignment/scope deterministically.

Specific mandatory assertions:

- `SYSTEM_ADMINISTRATOR` cannot view/approve ordinary business, screening, medical, compensation, classification, readiness, or export data through technical authority.
- `AUDITOR_READ_ONLY` cannot create/edit/review/approve/manage and remains assignment bounded.
- Recruiter cannot read restricted screening/medical contents or obtain them through parameters/projections.
- Candidate cannot receive/use a staff role.
- Staff cannot receive/use the candidate policy role.
- No wildcard, prefix match, `isAdmin`, missing permission, or unknown condition grants access.

## 11. Scope Matrix

Test every scope type with inside, outside, missing, inactive, expired, malformed, wrong-type, and cross-organization resources.

### 11.1 Assigned records

- Explicit currently assigned resource allows only when permission/other facts pass.
- Unassigned, formerly assigned, assigned to another user, or missing relationship denies.
- Assignment identifier/request claim cannot forge membership.

### 11.2 Team, branch, and organization

- Team contains only resolved team records.
- Branch contains its resolved child teams/records, not sibling/other branches.
- Organization contains only resolved descendants and never crosses organization.
- Client-selected scope may narrow but never widen.
- Missing/cyclic/type-mismatched ancestry denies.
- Wider scope on one role cannot broaden a narrow specialist permission from another role.

### 11.3 Audit assignment

- Enforce active assignment, record group, date range, event/data category, field sensitivity, and allowed purpose.
- Inclusive/exclusive time boundaries use the accepted policy exactly.
- Expired, future, revoked, wrong-category, wrong-date, wrong-record, and missing assignment denies/filter before materialization.

### 11.4 Synthetic scope boundary

Because M2 organization/candidate tables do not yet exist, use the accepted M1.4 deterministic resolver and synthetic authorization envelopes. Do not create fake production M2 tables/endpoints.

## 12. Object and Candidate Cross-Record Matrix

Using synthetic resource envelopes and current M1 owned account/session resources, prove:

- Candidate A can access only Candidate A's permitted self/security resources.
- Candidate A cannot access, infer, search, count, enumerate, mutate, download, or obtain timing/error distinctions for Candidate B.
- Changing only the opaque identifier while keeping Candidate A's valid session denies safely.
- Invalid-format, nonexistent, wrong-type, other-owner, out-of-scope, and hidden identifiers use equivalent external behavior where required.
- Staff wrong-scope object access denies before restricted payload materialization.
- Administrator possession of an identifier does not grant business access.
- Own-candidacy/worker-file conflict denies staff access/approval regardless of another role.
- Direct routes/actions deny even if navigation/control is hidden.

Candidate/business records remain synthetic policy fixtures until M2. Do not claim real candidacy authorization E2E complete.

## 13. Field and Projection Matrix

Test each classification and policy outcome:

| Classification | Required tests |
| --- | --- |
| `PUBLIC` | Include only approved public fields |
| `INTERNAL` | Exclude from candidate; include only permitted staff projection |
| `CONFIDENTIAL_PERSONNEL` | Scope/permission required; list remains minimal |
| `RESTRICTED_IDENTITY_FINANCIAL` | Separate permission, masking/default omission, recent auth for full access |
| `RESTRICTED_SCREENING_MEDICAL` | Specialist permission, separate read/download, excluded from general search |
| `SECURITY_AUDIT_RESTRICTED` | Narrow audit/security permission and scoped redacted projection |

For each representative resource/view purpose, test:

- `INCLUDE`, `MASK`, `STATUS_ONLY`, `OMIT`, and `DENY_RESOURCE` outcomes.
- List, detail, edit, status, search, audit, download/export contract variants where a contract exists.
- Unknown/new field metadata fails closed and exact response schema rejects drift.
- Never-return secret fields remain absent for every role and state.
- `null`, omission, masked, and status-only forms do not reveal restricted-value existence.
- Persistence/auth/provider objects cannot be spread/serialized into responses or client props.
- Client-selected fields/includes/projection names cannot widen output.
- Mass-assignment attempts for server-owned fields reject and cause no state/audit success event.
- Protected cache/RSC/hydration/client bundle/loading/error outputs contain no hidden fields.

Use synthetic restricted values/canaries only and do not print them on failure.

## 14. Workflow-State Matrix

M1 has no hiring aggregates. Test the accepted M1.4 named workflow policy interface with synthetic closed states:

- Every allowed state for each named policy permits only when all other facts pass.
- Every disallowed/terminal/superseded/expired state denies.
- Unknown/missing state or policy version denies.
- Client state claims are ignored.
- State changed between initial view and command transaction causes recheck and safe conflict/denial.
- Optimistic version mismatch never commits mutation/audit success.

Do not implement application/interview/offer/screening/onboarding/training/readiness aggregates to satisfy these tests.

## 15. Separation-of-Duties Matrix

Test immutable actor IDs, not display names or roles.

At minimum prove:

1. Actor cannot approve their own classification proposal.
2. Candidate cannot approve/verify their own screening, onboarding, training, competency, or readiness.
3. Restricted-result entrant cannot make final disposition when dual review applies.
4. Signed evaluation cannot be edited in place; correction policy requires version/amendment.
5. System administrator cannot perform business approvals.
6. Actor cannot approve their own compensation/offer change when dual approval applies.
7. Candidate and recruiter-only actor cannot approve final readiness.
8. Staff cannot access/approve their own candidacy/worker file.
9. Actor cannot modify audit records documenting that actor; no one has ordinary audit mutation authority.
10. Break-glass policy, if only represented as a denied/deferred policy, cannot approve ordinary workflow actions.
11. Role assignment subject cannot create/approve/widen/extend/reactivate their own authority.
12. Two-person approval rejects the same user even when acting under two different roles/assignments.

For each rule, test valid different-actor control, same actor, missing facts, swapped IDs, and multi-role cases. Use policy fixtures only for future workflows.

For every current M1 route/Server Action/Route Handler/auth endpoint:

- Public/unauthenticated behavior.
- Correct candidate/staff authenticated behavior.
- Wrong principal/account type.
- Inactive/incomplete/stale account/session.
- Direct invocation without navigation.

- CSRF/origin behavior for browser mutation.
- Bounded input/unknown-field/mass-assignment behavior.

## 17. Audit Matrix

For each required current M1 occurrence, assert:

- Correct audit versus security stream.
- Registered event name/schema/category/outcome.
- Server-owned actor/effective role/scope/permission/action/target/source/time/correlation context.
- Required safe reason and record versions.
- Exact metadata allowlist and absence of prohibited data.
- Exactly one event for one accepted command/required denial.
- Retry/idempotency behavior.
- Mutation plus audit atomic commit/rollback.
- Denial remains denial if event append fails.
- Runtime cannot update/delete/truncate/bypass history.
- Integrity chain remains valid and verifier detects controlled disposable corruption.
- Query projection authorization/redaction/date/category/assignment bounds.
- Audit-query event is recursion safe.

At minimum cover account registration/verification/recovery/security changes; sign-in/out/session changes; staff invitation/activation/MFA/backup/recovery; account restriction; role/scope/catalog/version changes; restricted current M1 access; high-risk denials; and integrity-verification failure.

## 18. Revocation and Race Matrix

Use real PostgreSQL transactions and deterministic barriers for:

- Account disable during a protected read/action.
- Role revocation during authorization/command commit.
- Assignment expiry exactly at the controlled boundary.
- Scope supersession during a query/action.
- Session revocation during a second request.
- Password reset/MFA reset while other sessions/challenges exist.
- Invitation acceptance concurrently/replayed.
- Backup code concurrently/replayed.
- Duplicate role grant/approval.
- Same-chain audit appends and forced audit failure.
- Reauthentication followed by privilege/resource change.

Exactly one valid outcome may commit where operations conflict. A stale browser/session/client claim never preserves authority. Losing requests receive safe denial/conflict without partial state or false audit success.

## 23. Flake, Isolation, and Test Hygiene

- No arbitrary sleeps as the primary synchronization mechanism.
- Inject/control clock, random/ID generators, TOTP time, email delivery, and concurrency barriers.
- One isolated database/schema and browser/email state per worker or an equivalent proven cleanup model.
- Tests do not depend on order or leaked global mocks.
- Parallel-safe tests may run in parallel; serialize only documented chain/database cases.
- Failed cleanup is a test failure.
- Retry is not used to hide deterministic defects; CI retry policy and first-attempt failure remain visible.
- Track suite duration and flake evidence across consecutive runs.
- Do not quarantine authorization/security/audit critical tests to pass the gate.
- Tests never call production networks or use production credentials/data.

## 26. Defect Handling

When the matrix exposes a defect:

- Record the failing requirement/matrix row and severity.
- Fix only within accepted M0–M1 scope when the correction is clear and does not change approved architecture/policy.
- Add the regression case before or with the fix.
- Rerun the focused suite, affected integration/E2E layers, and then restart both full gate runs.
- Stop for approval if the fix changes role grants, scope semantics, authentication assurance, audit schema/integrity, database migration, external behavior, or M2+ scope.
- No Critical or High authentication/authorization/audit/accessibility defect may be deferred for the M1 pass.
- Do not weaken an assertion, broaden a permission, mask an error, add retries, skip a test, or change expected output merely to obtain green CI.
